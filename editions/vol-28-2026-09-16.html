Physicalism

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy

Physicalism asserts a provocative metaphysical claim: everything is ultimately physical. While once distinguished from materialism as a linguistic thesis by Vienna Circle positivists, the terms are now largely interchangeable. Despite the existence of non-physical phenomena like psychology or morals, physicalists insist these elements fundamentally relate to physical reality, tethering metaphysics directly to the physical sciences.

The Vault - Hidden History

The Temples of Asclepius

In ancient Greece, healing was not outsourced to a sterile bureaucracy; it was confronted in the dark. At the Asclepeion temples, patients seeking cures for physical and psychological ailments engaged in incubation—a ritual sleep in the sacred abaton. They did not pray for magical external interventions. Instead, they actively sought out terrifying, vivid dreams where the god Asclepius would appear to deliver harsh truths or direct medical instructions. The ancients understood what modern physicalism and outsourced medicine try to deny: that true healing often requires a deliberate descent into our own subconscious shadows. Just as today's research reveals dreaming as a channel for deep processing and navigating anxieties, the Greeks knew that confronting our internal monsters is the only way to awaken whole. The cure is not avoiding the nightmare; the cure is paying attention to it.
